Vehicle Restorations

2022 – 2025

After completing Grade 11, I had the opportunity to purchase three classic vehicles from an estate sale that had been sitting exposed to the ocean air for over 30 years. Over the next several years, I executed comprehensive mechanical, electrical, and cosmetic restorations on two of them: a 1973 Mercedes-Benz 450SL (R107) and a 1976 Rolls-Royce Silver Shadow. Working from archival factory schematics and service manuals, this multi-year project focused on systematic fault isolation, component-level rebuilding, and CAD-based reverse engineering to return both vehicles to reliable, roadworthy operation.

1973 Mercedes-Benz 450SL (R107)

The 450SL restoration was executed in two distinct phases: an initial mechanical, electrical, and suspension overhaul to make the vehicle mechanically reliable, followed by a bare-metal cosmetic restoration and interior reconditioning.

Phase 1 — Initial Teardown & Mechanical Revival

After three decades of sitting outside, every major subsystem required attention before the engine could safely turn over or the car could be driven:

  • Fuel System: Rebuilt the K-Jetronic fuel pump, accumulator, and filter assembly; cleaned and replaced the fuel tank and lines; and rebuilt the fuel injectors.
  • Starting & Charging: Installed a new battery, starter motor, starter solenoid, and alternator.
  • Ignition & Fluids: Replaced spark plugs, flushed the cooling system, and changed all engine and transmission fluids.
  • Initial Braking & Electrical: Replaced brake pads, rotors, and lines, and repaired corroded factory wiring, fuses, and relays.

First Drive & Road Testing

Taking the 450SL for its first drive was a major milestone, but real-world road testing immediately revealed play in the steering linkage, degraded suspension bushings, and spongy brake feel—guiding the next round of mechanical work.

Suspension & Braking Overhaul

To eliminate vague steering and restore firm pedal feel, the suspension and braking systems were completely overhauled. Worn control arm bushings, steering couplings, ball joints, and tie rods were replaced. All brake calipers were disassembled and rebuilt with new pistons and seals, a new master cylinder was installed, and the entire system was bled.

Electrical Diagnostics & Cabin Rewiring

Using original factory wiring schematics, I traced and repaired the cabin and engine-bay harnesses. I upgraded the ignition system with an electronic ignition module, rewired the radio and speaker system, and replaced faulty fuses and relays to restore all interior instrumentation and exterior lighting.

CAD Reverse-Engineering & 3D Printing

Where original interior trim and climate-control hardware were broken or discontinued, components were measured, reverse-engineered in CAD, and 3D-printed—including replacement A/C control levers and custom mounting hardware, alongside a full surface model of the R107 body for a 3D-printed keychain.

Phase 2 — Bare-Metal Bodywork & Refinishing

Once mechanical reliability was proven, the car entered Phase 2: a bare-metal cosmetic restoration in factory MB Paint Code 904 (Midnight Blue). Every removable panel was detached, and the entire shell was stripped to bare metal by hand to remove surface oxidation and ensure proper primer adhesion.

I performed all surface preparation in-house—including dent removal, body filler shaping, panel gap alignment, block sanding, and primer application—before handing the prepped shell and panels to a professional paint booth for the final basecoat and clearcoat application.

Interior Reconditioning & Final Assembly

Simultaneously, the cabin and trunk were stripped and restored. The seats were reupholstered, and the dashboard, gauge cluster, door cards, and carpets were reconditioned. Final assembly reunited the painted panels, chrome bumpers, exterior trim, lighting harnesses, and a new convertible soft top with the chassis.

1976 Rolls-Royce Silver Shadow

The second restoration focused on a left-hand-drive 1976 Rolls-Royce Silver Shadow—an interim "Shadow 1.5" model incorporating Series II mechanical revisions while retaining the Series I body and dashboard architecture. The primary engineering challenge was diagnosing and rebuilding its seized, dual-redundant high-pressure hydraulic system and complex electrical architecture.

High-Pressure Hydraulic System Rebuild

The Silver Shadow's hydraulic system is a complex network of pumps, accumulators, height-control valves, and brake distribution valves designed to produce a linear brake pedal response alongside an active, self-leveling suspension:

  • Camshaft Pumps & Accumulators: Two camshaft-driven plunger pumps pressurize fluid to 2,500 psi inside two nitrogen-charged accumulator spheres, which act as "hydraulic batteries" to absorb pump pulsations and store reserve pressure for up to 60 brake applications.
  • Dual Redundancy & 6 Brake Calipers: Two independent high-pressure circuits remain physically isolated all the way to the wheels via six brake calipers—dual calipers on each front rotor and split dual-circuit calipers on the rear.
  • Linear Pedal Feel & Self-Leveling: Under-floor distribution valves use reaction pistons and progressive springs to translate 2,500 psi into a traditional, linear pedal feel, while mechanical height-control valves automatically meter fluid to rear suspension rams to keep the car level under load.

When these high-pressure hydraulic systems fail, the prohibitive cost of specialist labor and replacement parts causes many Silver Shadows to be abandoned or scrapped. However, the primary barrier to restoring the system is understanding its complex, interconnected functionality. After sitting for 30 years, much of the network was seized. By studying the factory fluid-power schematics, I isolated the faults on my Silver Shadow to the accumulators, brake distribution valves, and all six brake calipers. Each of these assemblies was completely disassembled, cleaned, rebuilt with new internal seals, and bled with fresh RR363 fluid—returning the system to factory operation.

Electrical System Diagnostics & Wiring

Much like its hydraulics, the Silver Shadow's electrical system is an intricate network of relays, fuses, limit switches, and multi-circuit harnesses. After decades of sitting in coastal humidity, the electrical system required systematic fault isolation. Using the factory wiring schematics, I traced the harnesses, replaced corroded relays, fuses, and switches, and restored operation across the ignition, lighting, power accessories, and climate control systems.
