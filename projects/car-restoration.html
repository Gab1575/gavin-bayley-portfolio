Car Restoration

2022 – 2025
System DiagnosticsElectricalBodywork Fusion 360Mechanics3D Printing Hydraulics

Procured three cars abandoned on the oceanfront for over 30 years and restored two of them to safe driving condition: a 1973 Mercedes 450SL and a 1976 Rolls-Royce Silver Shadow. Both came with obsolete manuals, 50-year-old wiring, and decades of deferred maintenance — most of what follows was learned by taking things apart, finding out why they failed, and putting them back together correctly.

1973 Mercedes 450SL

Deciphered obsolete manuals and 50-year-old wiring diagrams to learn and perform the repairs needed to get it safely back on the road. The rebuild happened in two passes: first a mechanical revival to get the car running and driving safely on its original paint, and later a full cosmetic rebuild — stripped to bare metal, resprayed, and reassembled.

Acquisition & First Diagnosis

The 450SL had been sitting outdoors for decades: oxidized single-stage paint, a disassembled front clip, and a fuel and electrical system that hadn't been touched in years. Before any cosmetic work, the front end came apart to check what was actually salvageable underneath — grille, headlight buckets, and bumper off, engine bay exposed for a full inspection.

Coaxing It Back to Life

Before touching bodywork, the priority was proving the drivetrain still worked. That meant going through the ignition system, the fuel delivery system, and the wiring harness connector by connector — cleaning corroded Bosch relay contacts, rebuilding the K-Jetronic fuel accumulator/pump assembly on the bench, and running the engine on the driveway with the hood up before committing to anything else. The clips below are from that testing period — cranking it over, checking for spark and fuel pressure, and finally letting it idle under its own power for the first time in years.

First Drive

Once the engine ran reliably, the car went back on the road on its original 50-year-old paint — patina and all. Before any decision was made to strip and repaint it, the priority was confirming it was safe and fun to actually drive.

Getting the Radio & Speakers Working

The factory harness under the dash had been cut into by a previous owner's aftermarket radio install. Getting audio working again meant tracing that wiring back against the factory diagram (below, in the Electrical section), re-terminating the harness properly instead of just splicing over the existing damage, and re-mounting a control unit that had been left taped in place.

Suspension Work

Pulling the front suspension apart to inspect it turned up exactly what 50 years outdoors does to a strut, coil spring, and brake rotor: heavy surface corrosion on every unprotected steel component. Everything came off, got assessed piece by piece, and went back together with new hardware where the originals weren't trustworthy anymore.

Prepping for Paint

With the car mechanically sound, the second pass — the cosmetic rebuild — started with picking a color. The factory offered the R107 SL in over 100 shades across its production run; the original color chart from SL Shop was used to pick the replacement, a period-correct dark blue-green. Every panel then came off and got stripped to bare metal by hand.

Paint

Panels went into the booth separately — easier to get even coverage on each one than to fight the whole shell at once. The body followed once the individual panels were done, masked off and sprayed in the same dark blue-green pulled from the SL Shop chart.

Putting It Back Together

Reassembly meant every panel, every piece of trim, and every wiring connector that had come off during teardown going back on in the right order. The grille and front bumper went back on last — the first sign the car was close to done was seeing the headlights light up again behind a fully assembled nose.

Interior Restoration

The interior was stripped, cleaned, and reconditioned alongside the bodywork — leather seats, dash, door cards, carpet, and the trunk, all brought back to a usable, presentable condition rather than replaced outright.

Electrical & Wiring Diagram

All of the electrical work above — the radio rewire, the relay cleanup, and the general fault-finding — was done against the factory wiring diagram for the 107-chassis SL/SLC, component list and wire color code included.

Design Integration

Used CAD to reverse-engineer and manufacture broken components, as well as a 3D-printed keychain and modeled AC controls.

1976 Rolls-Royce Silver Shadow

Completed a full mechanical restoration, including a complete tear-down and rebuild of the high-pressure hydraulic system used for braking and active suspension. Due to the complexities of this system, improper maintenance often results in these vehicles ending up in a junkyard — learning to repair components instead of replacing them, and understanding each component's place in the system, allowed a full rebuild into functioning condition. A more detailed, photo-by-photo write-up of this build is in progress.

Glam Shots

Once both cars were on the road, they made for a good side-by-side — a 1970s Mercedes SL and a Rolls-Royce Silver Shadow, both privately restored, a couple of decades apart in character but built in the same era.
