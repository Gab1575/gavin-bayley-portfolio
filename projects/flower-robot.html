LLM-Driven Mental Health Companion Robot

Undergraduate Research Assistant, MITHRIL Lab · Ingenuity Labs Research Institute, Queen's University · Supervised by Prof. Matthew Pan · May – Aug 2026
ROS 2micro-ROSESP32 / C++ PythonOpenCVFusion 360 TkinterHuman-Robot Interaction
[ Hero photo: the fully assembled flower robot ]

Designed, fabricated, and programmed a tendon-driven, continuum-stem animatronic flower robot from the ground up, serving as the physical embodiment for an LLM-driven mental health companion research platform in the lab's Human-Robot Interaction research program.

Mechanical Design

CAD in Fusion 360, laser-cut acrylic subframes, and 3D-printed structural parts, including a 4-tendon continuum stem built from stacked ball-and-socket joints and a five-petal head driven by its own base motor.

Software Architecture

Built the full ROS 2 (Humble) host stack: a Tkinter control GUI with a live 3D kinematics preview, a forward-kinematics solver mapping requested bend targets into antagonistic servo commands, and a command-mux layer bridged to the robot over micro-ROS/Wi-Fi.

Embedded Systems

Developed the ESP32 firmware (PlatformIO/C++) driving 4 servos through a PCA9685 I2C controller, WS2812B petal LEDs, and an encoder-controlled base motor, with a self-healing Wi-Fi/micro-ROS reconnection protocol using mDNS discovery.

Perception

Implemented two OpenCV-based perception nodes — HSV-segmentation ball localization and RetinaFace face tracking — to lay the groundwork for closed-loop and attention-based behaviors.

Documentation

Authored a 27-page technical handbook covering assembly, tuning, software architecture, and setup/operation, so the build is fully reproducible by future lab members.
